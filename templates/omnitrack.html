01 / Project Overview

OmniTrack is a wearable medical device prototype developed as part of an engineering design project at the University of Colorado Boulder. The project explored how modular mechanical components could be integrated into a compact wearable system while considering usability, comfort, and protection.

My work involved developing CAD models for a three-component design consisting of protective housing, an internal mechanism, and a wearable attachment system.

02 / Engineering Challenge

Wearable medical devices must balance multiple engineering considerations. A device should protect its internal components while remaining comfortable, accessible, and practical for everyday use.

For OmniTrack, the design challenge involved exploring a modular structure that could accommodate internal components and support secure attachment to the body.

03 / Design & Development

01 — Protective Housing

Developed CAD geometry for an external protective structure, considering enclosure dimensions, component placement, and durability.

02 — Internal Mechanism

Modeled the internal component arrangement with attention to spatial organization, assembly, and integration within the housing.

03 — Wearable Attachment

Explored a patch-based attachment concept intended to support secure placement and user comfort.

04 / CAD Modeling Workflow

I used SolidWorks and Onshape to develop three-dimensional models and explore the relationship between individual components and the overall wearable assembly.

  • Developed sketches and three-dimensional component geometry.
  • Considered component dimensions, alignment, and assembly requirements.
  • Explored modular construction to support design iteration.
  • Applied ergonomic and user-centered design considerations.

05 / Engineering Considerations

Ergonomics

Considering how wearable geometry and attachment affect user comfort.

Protection

Exploring enclosure design to protect internal components.

Modularity

Organizing the design into separate components to support assembly and iteration.

Accessibility

Considering usability for individuals who depend on wearable medical technology.

06 / Project Outcomes

The project provided hands-on experience in wearable device conceptualization, modular CAD modeling, and engineering design.

It strengthened my understanding of how mechanical design decisions influence component integration, user experience, and the practical development of medical technologies.

07 / Future Development

Potential next steps include evaluating material selection, prototyping the enclosure, assessing attachment comfort, and testing assembly and mechanical durability.

These activities represent future design opportunities, not completed validation testing.
