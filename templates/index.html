THE STORY BEHIND MY WORK

About Me

From Patient to Innovator: Engineering Beyond Limitations

My journey into biomedical engineering began at the age of five, when I was diagnosed with diabetes. Growing up, I experienced firsthand the challenges of managing a chronic medical condition through daily glucose monitoring and insulin injections.

As medical technology advanced, I witnessed the transition from traditional finger-prick glucose monitoring to continuous glucose monitors (CGMs) and insulin pumps. These innovations transformed my perspective on what engineering could accomplish.

"I no longer wanted to simply benefit from medical innovation. I wanted to become someone who creates it."

Today, as a biomedical engineering student, I am passionate about combining science, engineering, and human-centered design to develop technologies that address complex healthcare challenges.

My interests include medical device development, neural engineering, biomechanics, biomaterials, regenerative medicine, and CAD design. I am particularly interested in technologies that could improve mobility, rehabilitation, and independence for individuals with spinal cord injuries.

My experience as a patient has taught me that behind every medical device is a person whose quality of life may depend on its success. I hope to bridge the gap between patient experience and engineering innovation by developing technologies that genuinely improve lives.
